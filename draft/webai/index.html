03 THE STRUCTURE OF THE WEB

A web with
an uneven structure.

The web is a network of domains connected by links. Some domains sit close to its highly connected core; most are much farther out.

AN ILLUSTRATIVE NETWORK

Follow the links

PageRank considers both how many domains link to a site and how connected those linking domains are. A link from a highly connected domain can carry more weight. Highly central domains include major platforms and institutions; less-central domains include local organizations, small businesses, and personal sites.

Select a site to see the links pointing to it. This small network illustrates the idea; it is not a map of actual websites.

06 HOW WE MEASURED

Detection is
not certainty.

AI detectors make mistakes. They can flag human writing and miss text that involved AI. Their errors also change with the kind of text being measured.

Carry uncertainty forward

The model estimates detector performance and prevalence together. It allows error rates to vary with context and carries uncertainty about those rates into the reported intervals. A detector result for one page does not establish who wrote it.

The idea behind the weighted estimate ＋

For each crawl, the overall estimate averages estimated prevalence across groups of similar pages, weighted by each group’s share of eligible pages.

Estimated share = ∑ (group share × estimated prevalence in group)

08 THE BOUNDARY OF THE STUDY

What remains
outside the frame.

These results describe a large part of the public, crawlable web. They do not cover everything people see or read online.

01

Pages a crawler cannot reach

Pages behind logins, in apps, or on sites that block crawlers are outside the study. This includes much social-platform content.

02

Very short text

Pages needed at least 500 characters of extracted text. A collection of short posts can qualify as a page, but it is scored as a whole.

03

Changing coverage

Common Crawl does not capture the whole web uniformly, and its coverage and crawling practices can change over time.

04

Uncertain measurement

Results depend on detector benchmarks and model assumptions. Performance may vary across languages and change as writing tools evolve. The model does not explicitly account for additional similarity among pages on the same domain.

↗ ABOUT THE RESEARCH

About the data

The analysis uses approximately 5.1 million eligible pages from 71 Common Crawl snapshots between 2019 and August 2026. The outcome includes text generated or revised with language models. Estimates are weighted to eligible pages and include model-based uncertainty.

Open Pangram was applied to eligible pages; Binoculars was also applied to English pages. The model uses benchmarks and earlier controls to account for detector error. See the manuscript for full methods, assumptions, and limitations.

Read the data notes ＋

Intervals are 95% credible intervals unless otherwise noted. Network centrality refers to the domain’s PageRank in the Common Crawl web graph; it is not a measure of readership. Each page inherits its domain’s tier and top-level domain category. The plotted language categories are English only, no English detected, and English plus another language; pages of unknown language are excluded from those three lines.

Data on this page come from the matched sp_full model run. Earlier crawls are treated as low-prevalence controls, an assumption that informs rather than proves their origin. The common-composition estimates compare model predictions under a shared distribution of page characteristics and are not causal effects.
